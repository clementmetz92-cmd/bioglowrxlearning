About and accuracy

Short version: free study games written in our own words from real university textbooks, made by a small family team. We fix confirmed mistakes quickly, and we tell you which textbook each subject follows so you can check it against your course.

Who makes it

BioglowRX Sciences Learning is made by Clement Metz (project manager, who builds the site) and Aeneas Metz (health-sciences student, who plays and checks the games against her course). It started as a game to help Aeneas learn the periodic table and grew chapter by chapter. Contact: clementmetz92@gmail.com.

Everything is written in our own words. No text, figures or questions are copied from the books; chapter numbers are there so you can find the matching chapter in your own copy.

How the content is checked

  • Each game is written from its textbook chapter, then checked against the chapter and play-tested by Aeneas.
  • Before releases, automated tests open and play every game and mode on phone and desktop to catch broken questions and layouts.
  • Every question has a Report a mistake button. We read every report and fix confirmed mistakes quickly; fixes are listed below.
  • We are looking for a qualified subject expert to review each subject. Their name will appear here.

Units and names

Units and drug names follow the textbooks above, which are American: blood sugar in mg/dL, weights sometimes in pounds, and US generic drug names (for example acetaminophen, known as paracetamol in the UK and Europe). Where it matters, the explanation gives both. UK and European units side by side are planned.

Certificates and awards

Progress certificates, subject awards, levels and trophies are a fun record of your own practice. They are not academic qualifications, course credits or proof of competence, and no school or university issues them.

How the site is organised: each subject (such as Pharmacology) has chapters that follow the textbook, and each chapter has a set of activities: quizzes, case files, calculators and more. About four of them are core activities. A chapter's progress certificate needs every core activity passed at 70% or more and at least 30 of its questions answered; the other activities are optional extra practice.

Not medical advice

The games are for learning and exam practice. They are not medical advice and must not be used to make decisions about real patients or your own health. Always follow your course, your clinical instructors and local policy.
